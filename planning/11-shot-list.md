# Shot list — one page, record without improvising

**Runtime at 130 wpm: 4:54.** Ceiling is 5:00. Cumulative marks below assume
that pace. If you finish a section early, pause rather than speeding up.

**Before you hit record:** browser at 1280×800 or wider, zoom ~110%, page loaded,
scrolled to the very top, and the animation **paused with the scrubber at 0**
(click Pause, then drag the scrubber fully left). Nothing should be moving when
recording starts.

## The one awkward move

In step 6 the paired bars sit **below** the scatter chart on the page, but the
narration wants the bars first. So: scroll past the chart, speak the two-day
comparison over the bars, then scroll **back up** to the chart. It is one short
upward scroll and it reads fine — but rehearse it, because hunting for the chart
on camera looks worse than the scroll itself.

If you would rather avoid it entirely, swap the first two paragraphs of §6 so
the chart comes first. The argument survives either order.

## Say these carefully

Flagged from a read-aloud pass. None are broken; all are places people stumble.

| Line | Why | Suggestion |
|---|---|---|
| "One thousand three hundred and eighty-four fires." | Six number-words in a row. The single most likely fluff in the script. | Say it slowly with a beat before it. **"Fourteen hundred fires"** is easier and still true if you prefer — but the exact figure is more convincing. |
| "NOAA's HYSPLIT" | Two acronyms back to back. | **"NO-ah's HISS-split."** Practise it twice. |
| "two things following the calendar track each other without either causing the other" | Abstract, and "the other… the other" repeats. Needs a breath mid-phrase. | Breathe after "calendar". Land on **"without either causing the other."** |
| "A negative control that looked convincing, and died when we significance-tested it." | "significance-tested" is a mouthful. | Optional: "…and died the moment we tested it properly." |
| "it drives enforcement, court directives, fines on individual farmers, every winter" | Four-item list, three commas. | Slow down. Beat between each. This is the Earth Forward line — it should feel deliberate. |
| "Ask whether a *change* in burning predicts a *change* in pollution" | The whole point rests on vocal stress landing on both "change"es. | Hit both. Without the stress the sentence means nothing. |
| "our reconstruction says the arriving air hadn't come from the burning belt at all" | "arriving air hadn't" is a consonant pile-up. | Slight pause after "says". |

## If you run long

At 130 wpm you have six seconds of headroom. If a rehearsal lands over 5:00:

1. Cut "The faint lines are seven alternative starting points — that's our
   uncertainty." (12 words, ~6s). The legend on screen already says it.
2. Cut "That's called pre-registering: it means you can't quietly go looking for
   the version that works." (16 words, ~7s) — only if you have already said
   "wrote down what would count as success before we ran it", which carries the
   same meaning in plain language.

**Do not cut** "Why this matters", the four-cell failure, the farmers beat, or
the artefact line. Those four carry Adherence, Technology and Learning.

---

## Two takes

Record it twice even if the first feels fine. Keep the second. The first take is
where you find out which sentences you cannot say.
